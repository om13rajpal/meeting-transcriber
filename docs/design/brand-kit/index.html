Manifesto
A meeting is the only kind of work that disappears the moment it finishes. This product does one thing: it takes the words that were actually said, in the language they were actually said in, and puts them on the record where you can find them again. We do not summarise, we do not advise, and we do not read your recordings for any purpose other than transcribing them.

What this brand will do

  • Show the transcript. The real output is the hero graphic, not a stock illustration of a robot.
  • Print Hinglish as Hinglish. Code-switching mid-sentence is the normal case here, not an edge case.
  • Name the model and the price. Nova-3, and the exact dollar figure, on every completed meeting.
  • Say what failed and why, in the words a person can act on.
  • Get quiet the moment you sign in. The loud version is for the front door only.

What this brand will not do

  • Say "AI-powered". It is a speech model with a name and a per-minute rate.
  • Promise insight, action items, or intelligence it does not produce.
  • Use a gradient, a glow, a glass panel, or a floating 3D orb.
  • Soften a delete. If something is about to be gone, the copy says gone.
  • Animate anything you will see a hundred times a day.

Non-text contrast

WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 on UI component boundaries and meaningful graphics. The focus ring is --cr-red at 4.63:1 against the base canvas, which clears it. Input borders (--cr-rule-strong, 1.44:1) do not clear it on their own, which is why every field in this system pairs its border with a visible label and a focus ring rather than relying on the border to communicate the boundary. The 1px stamp border at --cr-red on paper is 3.70:1, which clears the graphic threshold even though the same colour fails as text.

Two voices, both free from Google Fonts, plus the body face the app already ships. Big Shoulders Display is condensed, heavy and always uppercase: it is the headline shouting across a courtroom. § 06 / Motion

Motion

This section is the contract. Three curves, eight durations, one press scale. Everything below is live: press it, hover it, open it. Use Slow motion 8x in the top bar to watch any of it frame by frame, and Reduced motion to see exactly what a person with that preference gets.

The four questions, answered for this product

1 · Should this animate at all?

Frequency decides. The dashboard search box is typed into dozens of times a day, so its results do not animate, they just change. The upload button is pressed a few times a day, so it gets press feedback. The landing hero is seen once, so it gets a timeline. Nothing keyboard-initiated animates, ever: Escape closing a dialog, Enter submitting a tag, Cmd+K. Those are pressed hundreds of times and animation makes them feel broken.

2 · What is the purpose?

Every animation here answers this in one word. Feedback: button press. Spatial consistency: the drawer leaves the way it came in. Explanation: the transcript stagger shows lines arriving the way a stenographer types them. Preventing a jarring change: the scroll reveal. If the answer is "it looks cool" and the user sees it daily, it does not ship.

3 · What easing?

Entering or exiting, use --cr-ease-out. Moving or morphing on screen, use --cr-ease-in-out. Drawers and sheets, use --cr-ease-drawer. That is the whole decision tree. Never ease-in on UI. It delays the first movement, which is the exact moment the user is watching, so a 200ms ease-in feels slower than a 200ms ease-out even though the clock says otherwise.

4 · How fast?

Press 140ms. Tooltip 160ms. Dropdown 200ms. Drawer 320ms. Modal 400ms. Product surfaces cap at 400ms. Marketing may go to 700ms for the hero timeline and 600ms for scroll reveals, because those are seen once by a person who is reading, not working. Perceived speed is real speed: a 180ms select feels more responsive than a 400ms one, and that difference is the entire reason for the cap.

The three curves

Verbatim, and locked. The built-in CSS easings are too weak to read as intentional. These three are the whole vocabulary. Adding a fourth is a design decision, not an implementation detail, and it needs review.

CSS or GSAP

CSS transitions · the default

Simple state changes: press, hover, focus, open, close. CSS runs off the main thread, so it stays smooth while Next.js is hydrating or the dashboard is polling. It is also interruptible and retargets mid-flight, which keyframes cannot do. Every component in section 07 uses CSS.

.btn {
  transition: transform 140ms var(--cr-ease-out);
}
.btn:active { transform: scale(0.97); }

GSAP · sequences and scroll

Reach for GSAP when there is a timeline (the hero), a stagger across a collection (transcript lines, dashboard rows), or anything scroll-driven (ScrollTrigger.batch). Do not reach for it to fade one element.

gsap.to(el, {
  scale: 0.97, duration: 0.14,
  ease: 'power3.out'
});

The ten hard rules

  1. Never animate a keyboard-initiated action. Escape, Enter, Cmd+K, arrow keys. Those fire hundreds of times a day.
  2. Never use ease-in on UI. It delays the frame the user is watching hardest.
  3. Never animate from scale(0). Start at 0.95 or higher, with opacity.
  4. Never use transition: all. Name the properties. all animates things you did not intend and pays for layout you did not need.
  5. Only animate transform and opacity. Width, height, margin and padding trigger layout on every frame.
  1. Popovers scale from their trigger. transform-origin: var(--transform-origin). Modals are the exception and stay centred.
  2. Exits are faster than entrances. The user has already decided; do not make them wait for the system to agree.
  3. Transitions, not keyframes, for anything that can be re-triggered mid-flight. Keyframes restart from zero; transitions retarget.
  4. Gate hover animations behind @media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine). Touch fires hover on tap.
  5. Reduced motion means fewer and gentler, not none. Keep opacity and colour, which aid comprehension. Remove travel, scale and draw.

All motion in this product is reviewed by emil-design-eng. Change an easing or a duration without consulting it and you are breaking the system.

§ 07 / Components

Components

Every component here maps to a shadcn/ui primitive that is already installed in this project. Court Reporter is applied by changing CSS variable values in app/globals.css, not by restyling components with className. If you find yourself writing className="bg-[#E63946]", the token map is wrong, not the component.

Three traps in this specific project

1 · This is Base UI, not Radix. Use render={<a href="..." />}, never asChild. Pass nativeButton={false} when a Button renders as an anchor. DropdownMenuLabel must sit inside a DropdownMenuGroup.
2 · shadcn's --accent is not the brand accent. In shadcn it is the subtle hover surface behind menu items and rows. Setting it to stamp red turns every dropdown hover into a red block. The brand red lands on --primary and --ring.
3 · Never use text-primary on dark. --primary is #B8202D, a surface colour, and measures 3.02:1 as text on the canvas. Red text uses --cr-red-text, which is what --destructive is set to.

§ 08 / Voice and tone

Voice and tone

Court Reporter writes the way a good stenographer talks: plain, specific, and completely uninterested in impressing you. Name the thing. Give the number. Never soften a consequence. The one place warmth is allowed is Hinglish, because that is how the people using this actually speak, and flattening it into corporate English would be its own kind of lie.

Do

Do not

Means nothing, cannot be verified, and invites a question the product cannot answer.

Do

§ 09 / Layout

Layout

Two column widths, ten spacing steps, four breakpoints. Court Reporter separates things with rules, not shadows. There are exactly two shadows in the whole system and both are marketing-only. If a product surface needs to show that one thing sits above another, it uses a 1px hairline and a background shift.

Column widths

1180px · marketing

Landing, auth, share. 24px gutters. The hero is a 1.05fr / 0.95fr two-column grid that collapses at 940.

1040px · app

Dashboard and MeetingDetail. Narrower on purpose: a list of meetings that runs 1180px wide makes every row feel emptier than it is.

Prose caps at 460px regardless of container. A lede that runs the full 1180 is unreadable no matter how good the type is.

Spacing scale

4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 56, 88, 120. Section padding is 88. Card padding is 24. Row padding is 16. Gaps inside a control are 8. Nothing lands between two steps.

Stroke scale

The border scale is one value: 1px. Emphasis is carried by the rule's colour, never by its weight. A quiet divider and a structural edge are the same hairline in two different colours, which is how a printed page does it.

v1 of this kit had a 2px emph and a 2.5px stamp weight and read as a stack of thick bordered boxes. Both are gone. A 1.5px middle tier was tried as a replacement and also removed, for a concrete reason: Blink floors sub-pixel border widths, so border: 1.5px computes and paints as 1px in Chrome at any device pixel ratio, while Safari and Firefox honour it. A tier that only exists in two of three engines is not a tier, it is an inconsistency. If a box seems to need more weight than a hairline, it does not need a heavier border: it needs a different background, or more space.

One weight, four colours

rule-soft List dividers, card and panel borders. The quietest, and the most used.
rule-strong Input borders, chip outlines, the time rail. A boundary you can act on.
paper Structural edges: the hero block's top and bottom, the stat triplet. Rationed.
red The accent edge. Section openers, the stamp outline, accent left-borders. The heaviest thing this system says, said at 1px.

Not borders, not on the scale

focus · 2px Accessibility affordance, and now the only line in the UI heavier than a hairline, which is exactly what a focus indicator should be. Never trade this down.
icon · 1.75px A drawn line, not a frame. Icons are content, so they sit above the chrome scale on purpose: the frame recedes, the mark does not.
ribbon · 34px The hand-off ribbon's artwork line weight. Illustration, not chrome, and unchanged.

Elevation

Rules · everywhere

Always 1px. What changes is the colour: --cr-rule-soft between list items, --cr-paper around a hero block, --cr-red to open a section. This is the default and it covers about 95% of cases.

Sheet shadow · marketing

0 50px 100px -30px rgba(0,0,0,.65). Used once, on the hero transcript card, because that card is literally a sheet of paper lying on a dark desk. The shadow is doing representational work, not decorative work.

0 14px 30px -12px rgba(184,32,45,.55). The landing primary button only. Never on the dashboard Upload button, which is the same component in a quieter room.
